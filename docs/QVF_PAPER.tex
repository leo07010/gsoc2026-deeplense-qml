\begin{abstract}
We present \textbf{QVF-Hybrid}, a hybrid quantum--classical architecture for
classifying dark-matter substructure in strong gravitational-lensing images,
built together with a control protocol designed to survive the fair-comparison
standards recently demanded of quantum machine learning
\cite{bowles2024,schnabel2025}.
A compact CNN encoder produces a 128-dimensional feature vector $\mathbf{h}$;
a quantum branch maps $\mathbf{h}$ through a learnable
\emph{Neural Amplitude Encoding} (NAE) into an 8-qubit state, applies a
\texttt{StronglyEntanglingLayers} circuit ($T{=}96$ trainable angles), and reads
out 52 Pauli observables $\mathbf{m}$; a residual head classifies
$\mathrm{concat}(\mathrm{LN}(\mathbf{h}), \mathrm{LN}(\mathbf{m}))$.
Zeroing the $\mathbf{m}$-block reduces the head \emph{exactly} to a plain-CNN
linear classifier (verified numerically, error $=0.0$), so the plain-CNN floor
holds \emph{by construction}.
Against three controls we report a three-tier characterization, each with its
statistical status made explicit.
\textbf{(1) Module level---vs.\ a parameter-matched sham} (circuit $\to$
Linear$+$Tanh, with \emph{more} parameters): a \emph{statistically significant}
low-data quantum contribution at the strongest cell (Model\_II, $N{=}250$),
confirmed under a pre-registered two-stage sequential design ($n{=}20$, one-sided
paired Wilcoxon, $p{=}0.0032 < \alpha{=}0.01$); its magnitude ($+1.4\%$ AUC)
nonetheless stays \emph{below} our $3\%$ practical bar, an ablation attributes it
to the amplitude-embedding structure and multi-basis readout rather than
entanglement, and its direction replicates on a third dataset (Model\_III,
$N{=}250$, $n{=}3$).
\textbf{(2) Model level---vs.\ a plain-CNN null baseline} (the smallest model in
the program, $93{,}763$ parameters): a \emph{constructive floor}---the hybrid is
never materially below plain across all tested cells---but the point estimate over
plain is not significant ($p{=}0.088$, $n{=}20$), so we make \emph{no} model-level
advantage claim.
\textbf{(3) Paradigm level---vs.\ the DeepLense MAE-ViT state-of-the-art}
($2{,}722{,}947$ parameters, self-supervised pretraining): the hybrid clears the
\emph{pre-registered} $\geq 3\%$ bar in 10 of 11 cells across three datasets
($3$-seed, $3/3$ seed-paired, $+3.1$ to $+32.0$ AUC points), and on three
representative low-$N$ cells this win is \emph{statistically significant}
($n{=}10$, Holm $p{=}0.003$, $10/10$ seed-paired, $+22$--$28$ AUC points), using
$1/19$ the parameters and \emph{zero} pretraining data.
We are explicit that the quantum contribution stays \emph{below} our own $3\%$
practical-significance bar, that the paradigm win belongs to the CNN
inductive-bias family rather than to the circuit, and we report the negative
results---wide-readout regression at mid-$N$, non-transferring qubit scaling, a
dead circuit-learning-rate lever, and topological-feature and raw-pixel quantum
failures---that bound the claim.
To our knowledge this is the most controlled characterization of where a small
quantum circuit does, and does not, help on gravitational-lensing classification.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Strong gravitational lensing is a promising probe of the substructure of
dark-matter halos~\cite{hezaveh2017fast,alexander2020deep}.
When light from a distant source passes near a massive foreground object,
spacetime curvature produces observable arc distortions and ring morphologies.
The subtle statistics of these distortions encode the foreground dark-matter
distribution: axion (ultralight) dark matter~\cite{hui2017ultralight},
cold dark matter (CDM) subhalos, and smooth (substructure-free) halos each leave
distinct---and difficult to disentangle---morphological imprints in
$64\times64$ lensing images.
Traditional inference methods based on flux-ratio
anomalies~\cite{dalal2002detecting} and gravitational
imaging~\cite{brehmer2019mining} are powerful but computationally prohibitive at
the scale of forthcoming surveys (Rubin LSST, Euclid, Roman Space Telescope),
which will deliver tens of thousands of strong-lensing events.
Reliably distinguishing these scenarios in the \emph{data-scarce} regime
(a few hundred labeled observations per class) is a central challenge, and it is
the regime in which the classical state-of-the-art is weakest.

Classical deep learning has made substantial progress on automated lens
classification. Convolutional
architectures~\cite{hezaveh2017fast,lanusse2018cmu,alexander2020deep},
simulation-based inference~\cite{brehmer2019mining}, and most recently
MAE-pretrained Vision Transformers~\cite{prasha2025masked} have pushed the
benchmark AUC above $0.97$ at full data.
Whether a \emph{quantum} circuit can add anything on top of such classical
pipelines is the question this paper attacks---and it attacks it under the
methodological microscope the field now requires.

\paragraph{The baseline crisis in quantum machine learning.}
A recurring lesson from the last two years of QML benchmarking is that reported
quantum advantages evaporate once the classical control is made honest.
Bowles et al.~\cite{bowles2024} show that many published hybrid quantum--classical
models are beaten by trivially small classical baselines once architecture and
parameter budget are matched; Schnabel et al.~\cite{schnabel2025} extend this to
a systematic study over thousands of model instances and find no robust quantum
edge on standard supervised tasks.
The failure mode is almost always the same: the ``quantum'' model is compared
against either (i) a much larger classical model it happens to beat for reasons
unrelated to the circuit, or (ii) a handicapped classical ``sham'' whose deficit
the circuit merely recovers.
A companion study of our own on Quantum Orthogonal Vision Transformers reaches an
identically cautionary conclusion---the orthogonal (RBS) attention circuit
\emph{matches but does not beat} full classical multi-head attention, and its
apparent low-data edge is an orthogonality regularizer, not a quantum
effect~\cite{qovt_companion,cherrat2022}.
Any credible quantum-contribution claim must therefore be defended against a
\emph{ladder} of controls, not a single favorable comparison.

\paragraph{Our contribution: a control ladder and an honest characterization.}
We do not claim quantum advantage.
Instead we build the strongest hybrid architecture we can for the quantum side,
subject it to a complete control ladder, and report precisely what survives.
Concretely:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A guaranteed-floor architecture.}
        QVF-Hybrid places the quantum branch \emph{in residual}: the classifier
        sees $\mathrm{concat}(\mathrm{LN}(\mathbf{h}),\mathrm{LN}(\mathbf{m}))$,
        and zeroing the quantum block recovers the plain-CNN linear classifier
        exactly (Proposition~\ref{prop:floor}). The plain-CNN null baseline is a
        \emph{lower bound by construction}, not by luck.
  \item \textbf{A statistically significant module-level quantum contribution
        vs.\ a parameter-matched sham.}
        On Model\_II at $N{=}250$ the residual quantum branch beats its sham by a
        margin that is significant under a pre-registered two-stage sequential
        test ($n{=}20$, $p{=}0.0032 < \alpha{=}0.01$); the effect is small
        ($+1.4\%$ AUC, below our $3\%$ practical bar), attributable to circuit
        structure rather than entanglement, and directionally replicated on
        Model\_III (Section~\ref{sec:significance}).
  \item \textbf{A large, partly significance-tested paradigm win over MAE-ViT.}
        With $1/19$ the parameters and no pretraining, QVF-Hybrid clears the
        \emph{pre-registered} $\geq 3\%$ bar over the MAE-ViT SOTA in 10 of 11
        cells across three datasets ($3$-seed, $3/3$ seed-paired, $+3.1$ to
        $+32.0$ AUC points); on three representative low-$N$ cells this win is
        statistically significant ($n{=}10$, Holm $p{=}0.003$, $10/10$
        seed-paired, $+22$--$28$ AUC points). We attribute the win to the CNN
        inductive-bias family, of which the hybrid is the strongest variant
        (Section~\ref{sec:maecomp}).
  \item \textbf{An entanglement ablation.}
        Under the literature's fair-comparison protocol
        (equal parameter shape, entangling vs.\ entanglement-free vs.\ classical),
        the entangling circuit strictly beats its Rot-only version, which strictly
        beats the linear sham, in 6 of 6 three-arm cells on the forced-readout
        architecture---an ordering that collapses to ENT $\approx$ NOENT once the
        residual bypass removes the bottleneck, localizing the residual benefit to
        amplitude-embedding structure rather than the entangling ring
        (Section~\ref{sec:entanglement}).
  \item \textbf{Negative results as first-class findings.}
        We document where the quantum side fails---wide readouts, qubit scaling
        on the residual architecture, learning-rate levers, topological input
        features, and raw-pixel quantum encoders---so the reader can see the
        boundary of the claim, not just its interior (Section~\ref{sec:negative}).
\end{enumerate}

The organizing theoretical fact, made precise in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}, is a
\emph{data-processing} constraint: the quantum branch is a deterministic function
of $\mathbf{h}$, so it can reshape the geometry the linear head sees but cannot
add label information the CNN has not already extracted. This predicts exactly
what we observe---a low-$N$ prior with real but bounded value, redundant at high
$N$---and it explains why the large module-level margins of our earliest
experiments were an artifact of a handicapping bottleneck rather than a genuine
quantum information gain.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Deep learning for gravitational lensing.}
Early CNN approaches to strong-lens classification established the feasibility of
automated morphological discrimination~\cite{hezaveh2017fast,lanusse2018cmu}.
Alexander et al.~\cite{alexander2020deep} demonstrated deep morphology learning
for dark-matter substructure, and Brehmer et al.~\cite{brehmer2019mining}
introduced simulation-based inference to marginalize over subhalo populations.
The current state-of-the-art~\cite{prasha2025masked} applies Masked-Autoencoder
(MAE) pretraining to a Vision Transformer, reaching AUC ${\approx}0.97$ at full
data but requiring $2.72\mathrm{M}$ parameters and a large-scale self-supervised
pretraining phase on unlabeled lensing images. It is the paradigm baseline we
target, and---crucially---the low-data frontier it underserves is where we test.

\paragraph{Honest benchmarking of quantum models.}
Our methodology follows the fair-comparison program of
Bowles et al.~\cite{bowles2024} and the large-scale null study of
Schnabel et al.~\cite{schnabel2025}, both of which demonstrate that a
capacity-matched classical control (a ``sham'') is the minimum bar for any
module-level quantum claim, and that isolated single-seed positives routinely
flip sign under replication.
We adopt three of their disciplines directly---capacity-matched shams,
multi-seed replication, and pre-registered success criteria---and add a
constructive-floor null baseline and an information-theoretic account of the
mechanism.

\paragraph{Quantum generalization theory.}
Foundational results establish that quantum models can generalize from fewer
samples than their expressivity might suggest.
Caro et al.~\cite{caro2022} prove that the generalization gap of a $T$-parameter
quantum circuit on $N$ samples is bounded by $C\sqrt{T/N}$, independent of
Hilbert-space dimension. Huang et al.~\cite{huang2021power} examine the
comparative advantage of quantum versus classical models as a function of data,
and Liu et al.~\cite{liu2021rigorous} give a provable quantum speed-up for
specific kernel classes, building on the feature-Hilbert-space view of quantum
models~\cite{schuld2019kernel}. We use the Caro bound as a design principle for the
\emph{parameter efficiency} of the quantum branch, while being careful
(Section~\ref{sec:analysis}) not to over-read it as a promise of advantage.

\paragraph{Hybrid quantum-classical architectures.}
Quanvolutional networks~\cite{henderson2020quanvolutional} and quantum
CNNs~\cite{cong2019quantum} established the hybrid paradigm.
Transfer learning~\cite{mari2020transfer} between classical and quantum components
showed that classical pretraining followed by quantum fine-tuning is viable.
Hybrid Quantum Vision Transformers have been applied to high-energy
physics~\cite{unlu2024hybrid,comajoan2024quantum,tesi2024quantum}, and orthogonal
(RBS) attention circuits to image
classification~\cite{cherrat2022}. Our own companion negative result on the
latter~\cite{qovt_companion}, together with quantum-MAE
studies~\cite{andrews2025qmae}, motivates the residual, guaranteed-floor design
here: rather than replacing a classical stage with a quantum one and hoping it
does not regress, we add the quantum branch \emph{in parallel} so that the worst
case is exactly the classical baseline.

\paragraph{Amplitude encoding.}
Efficient state preparation is a long-standing challenge in quantum
computing~\cite{mottonen2005transformation,grover2002creating}.
Zoufal et al.~\cite{zoufal2019qgan} used quantum GANs for distribution loading.
Wang et al.~\cite{wang2025} proposed \emph{Neural Amplitude Encoding}---a
learnable Boltzmann normalization that jointly trains an encoder and circuit---in
the context of quantum implicit neural representations~\cite{zhao2024quantum}.
We borrow the NAE state-preparation map only; our task (classification), circuit
ansatz, and readout differ from theirs.

\paragraph{Expressibility and trainability of PQCs.}
Sim et al.~\cite{sim2019expressibility} characterize PQC expressibility via
deviation from Haar-random distributions.
McClean et al.~\cite{mcclean2018barren} identify barren plateaus as the central
trainability bottleneck for deep quantum circuits, and
Cerezo et al.~\cite{cerezo2021cost} show that cost-function locality can mitigate
them. Schuld et al.~\cite{schuld2021effect} analyze how data encoding affects the
expressive power of variational models. Our training audit
(Section~\ref{sec:analysis}) verifies the absence of barren plateaus in the
8-qubit configuration used here.

final residual design is a considered response to specific failures rather than a

\subsection{Step 1 --- QVF-Scratch: the quantum readout head}
\label{sec:scratch}

\paragraph{Pipeline.}
The starting architecture, \emph{QVF-Scratch}, is a four-stage pipeline
$f_\theta : \mathbb{R}^{64\times64} \to \mathbb{R}^3$ trained end-to-end from
random initialization with no pretraining or external data:
\begin{equation}
  x
  \;\xrightarrow{\mathrm{CNN}}\;
  \mathbf{h} \in \mathbb{R}^{128}
  \;\xrightarrow{\mathrm{NAE}}\;
  \mathbf{a} \in \mathbb{R}^{256}
  \;\xrightarrow{\text{8-qubit PQC}}\;
  \mathbf{m} \in [-1,1]^{8}
  \;\xrightarrow{\mathrm{head}}\;
  \hat{y} \in \mathbb{R}^3.
  \label{eq:pipeline}
\end{equation}
All modules are trained jointly via PennyLane \texttt{backprop} (automatic
differentiation through the exact statevector).

\paragraph{CNN encoder.}
Three strided convolution blocks reduce $1\times64\times64$ to $\mathbf{h}$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{h}
  = \mathrm{AdaptiveAvgPool2d}(1)\!\left(
      \mathrm{ConvBnReLU}_{128} \circ
      \mathrm{ConvBnReLU}_{64}  \circ
      \mathrm{ConvBnReLU}_{32}(x)
    \right) \in \mathbb{R}^{128},
\end{equation}
each $\mathrm{ConvBnReLU}_c$ using kernel size $3$, stride $2$, padding $1$
($93{,}120$ parameters total).

\paragraph{Neural Amplitude Encoding.}
A two-layer energy MLP maps $\mathbf{h}$ to $256$ valid amplitudes via the
Boltzmann construction of~\cite{wang2025}:
\begin{align}
  E_\phi(\mathbf{h}) &= W_2 \tanh(W_1 \mathbf{h} + b_1) + b_2 \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{256}, \label{eq:energy}\\
  \mathbf{a}         &= \sqrt{\mathrm{softmax}(-E_\phi(\mathbf{h}))} + \varepsilon\mathbf{1},
                       \qquad \|\mathbf{a}\|_2 = 1, \label{eq:nae}
\end{align}
with $\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$. The $\sqrt{\mathrm{softmax}(-\cdot)}$ form guarantees
non-negativity and unit norm for \emph{any} $E_\phi$, so it never saturates the
\texttt{AmplitudeEmbedding} circuit.

\paragraph{Quantum circuit and measurement.}
The amplitudes are loaded as an 8-qubit state
$|\psi_0\rangle = \sum_{i=0}^{255} a_i |i\rangle$, then rotated by four
\texttt{StronglyEntanglingLayers} (SEL)~\cite{pennylane},
\begin{equation}
  |\psi_\mathrm{out}\rangle = U(\theta_\mathrm{PQC})\,|\psi_0\rangle,
  \qquad T = 3 \times 8 \times 4 = 96 \text{ angles,}
\end{equation}
each layer applying arbitrary single-qubit rotations followed by a CNOT
entanglement ring. The scratch readout is the vector of Pauli-$Z$ expectations
$\mathbf{m} = [\langle \hat{Z}_q\rangle]_{q=0}^{7} \in [-1,1]^{8}$, and the head is
$\hat{y} = W_\mathrm{cls}\,\mathrm{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{m}) + b_\mathrm{cls}$.
The \emph{sham} control replaces the PQC with $\mathrm{Linear}(256,8)+\mathrm{Tanh}$,
keeping every other module identical; the sham has \emph{more} parameters
($144{,}755$) than the quantum model ($142{,}795$).

\paragraph{The apparent win---and why it is not the end of the story.}
QVF-Scratch beats both its sham and the MAE-ViT SOTA at full data on all three
datasets (Table~\ref{tab:scratch-full}), and an 11-point data-size sweep
(single seed) found the quantum-over-sham margin positive at $21$ of $22$ points,
peaking at $\Delta{=}{+}0.112$ at $N{=}1500$/class on Model\_II. Taken alone, this is exactly
the kind of result the benchmarking literature warns against: a favorable
comparison against a handicapped sham and an oversized ViT. The rest of this
section is the ladder of controls that recontextualizes it.

\subsection{Step 2 --- The null baseline: a plain CNN Pareto-dominates}
\label{sec:tax}

The control a reviewer actually demands is neither a handicapped sham nor an
oversized ViT: it is a \emph{plain classical CNN with a direct readout}, same
encoder, same protocol, \emph{fewer} parameters. We built two:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{plain-lin}: $\mathrm{CNN}\to\mathrm{LayerNorm}\to\mathrm{Linear}(128,3)$,
        \textbf{$93{,}763$ params}---the smallest model in the entire program;
  \item \textbf{plain-mlp}: $\mathrm{CNN}\to\mathrm{Linear}(128,371)\to\mathrm{ReLU}
        \to\mathrm{LN}\to\mathrm{Linear}(371,3)$, $142{,}837$ params
        (capacity-matched to the quantum model).
\end{itemize}
Run under the identical protocol, the $93{,}763$-parameter plain-lin CNN
\emph{matches or beats every quantum variant} (QVF-scratch, and the QOVT variants
of the companion study) \emph{and} the $2.72\mathrm{M}$ MAE-ViT at essentially
every $(\text{dataset}, N)$. At $N{=}500$/class on Model\_II it beats QVF-scratch
quantum by $+0.092$---the very low-data regime where quantum regularization was
supposed to be strongest.

\paragraph{A three-column ledger.}
The apparent scratch win decomposes into a \emph{bottleneck tax} and a partial
\emph{recovery}. Forcing all $128$ CNN dimensions through the NAE$\,\to\,$circuit
readout costs information (tax); the quantum circuit recovers part of it over the
linear sham (recovery); the \emph{net} against the un-taxed plain CNN is what
matters. Table~\ref{tab:ledger} shows the ledger at the worst-tax cell.

\subsection{Step 3 --- QVF-Wide: widening the readout is not enough}
\label{sec:wide}

If the tax is a width problem, widening the readout should remove it. We widened
$\mathbf{m}$ from $8$ to $52$ observables
($8\langle Z\rangle + 8\langle X\rangle + 8\langle Y\rangle + 28\langle Z_iZ_j\rangle$),
head $\mathrm{LayerNorm}(52)\to\mathrm{Linear}(52,3)$; sham
$\mathrm{Linear}(256,52)+\mathrm{Tanh}$ ($156{,}283$ params vs.\ quantum
$143{,}015$). Table~\ref{tab:wide} shows the outcome (single-seed diagnostic).

\paragraph{Findings.}
(i) Widening helps exactly where the tax was worst: $+0.024$ over orig-Q at
$N{=}500$, quantum-vs-sham margin $+0.060$---but still $-0.068$ vs.\ plain.
(ii) It \emph{regresses} at mid-$N$ ($0.9631$ vs.\ orig $0.9808$ at $N{=}3000$),
and the sham \emph{beats} quantum at $N{=}8000$ ($-0.0045$): more observables are
not a free lunch; the $52$-dim head dilutes the $\langle Z\rangle$ signal that
carried the original margin.
The tax is therefore not merely readout \emph{width}---forcing all $128$ CNN
dimensions through the NAE$\to$circuit path is \emph{itself} the tax. This
directly motivates the residual design.

\subsection{Step 4 --- QVF-Hybrid: a guaranteed-floor residual head}
\label{sec:hybrid}

The final architecture keeps the CNN feature $\mathbf{h}$ on a \emph{bypass} and
adds the quantum readout $\mathbf{m}$ (the $52$-observable branch of
Section~\ref{sec:wide}) as a residual:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y} = W_\mathrm{head}\;
  \mathrm{concat}\!\big(\mathrm{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{h}),\,\mathrm{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{m})\big) + b_\mathrm{head},
  \qquad W_\mathrm{head}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 180}.
  \label{eq:hybrid-head}
\end{equation}
Write $W_\mathrm{head} = [\,W_h \mid W_m\,]$ with $W_h\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times128}$
acting on $\mathrm{LN}(\mathbf{h})$ and $W_m\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times52}$ on
$\mathrm{LN}(\mathbf{m})$.

\begin{proposition}[Plain-CNN floor by construction]
\label{prop:floor}
Setting $W_m = 0$ makes the QVF-Hybrid predictor
$\hat{y} = W_h\,\mathrm{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{h}) + b_\mathrm{head}$, which is
\emph{identically} the plain-lin classifier
$\mathrm{CNN}\to\mathrm{LayerNorm}\to\mathrm{Linear}(128,3)$.
Hence the QVF-Hybrid hypothesis class contains the plain-lin hypothesis class,
and the optimal QVF-Hybrid training risk is no larger than the optimal plain-lin
training risk. The quantum branch can only help or be ignored; it cannot make the
model worse than the plain CNN.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
The reduction is exact, not asymptotic: we verified numerically before launch
that zeroing the $\mathbf{m}$-block reproduces the plain-lin forward pass to
floating-point error $0.0$. Proposition~\ref{prop:floor} bounds the
\emph{training} objective; on held-out data the floor holds empirically within the
noise band across all 8 tested cells (Section~\ref{sec:hybrid-main}), with a worst
case of $-0.009$ AUC against the stronger of the two plain baselines.
\end{remark}

The quantum branch is unchanged from Section~\ref{sec:wide}
(NAE $\to$ 8-qubit SEL, $T{=}96$, $52$ observables). Table~\ref{tab:params}
gives the parameter budget of every arm; the quantum model uses fewer parameters
than its sham and than the MAE-ViT it is compared against.

\subsection{Controls Protocol}
\label{sec:controls}

Every comparison in this paper follows a fixed protocol, set before the runs and
applied identically to all arms.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Capacity-matched sham.} Each quantum result is paired with a sham
        in which the circuit is replaced by a classical $\mathrm{Linear}+\mathrm{Tanh}$
        of matched I/O dimension; the sham always has \emph{more} parameters than
        the quantum model ($156{,}923 \geq 143{,}655$), so no observed margin can be
        attributed to capacity~\cite{bowles2024}.
  \item \textbf{Bit-identical subsampling.} At each seed, the training subset is
        drawn with a fixed RNG (\texttt{seed}\,$+\,1000$) shared across all methods,
        giving \emph{bit-identical} training subsets per seed. We numerically
        verified this alignment on the MAE side so that the paradigm comparison
        (Section~\ref{sec:maecomp}) trains every method on exactly the same images.
  \item \textbf{Best-val-AUC checkpoint.} We report the checkpoint with the highest
        one-vs-rest macro-averaged validation AUC, never the last epoch.
  \item \textbf{Multi-seed replication.} All headline cells use $3$ seeds
        ($42/43/44$); we report the mean and the per-seed sign pattern.
  \item \textbf{Practical-significance bar.} We treat single-cell differences below
        $3\%$ AUC as within our noise band and do not claim them as effects; a
        margin is a ``win'' only if it clears $3\%$ \emph{and} is seed-consistent.
  \item \textbf{Pre-registration.} Success criteria (e.g.\ ``hybrid-Q beats
        $\max(\text{plain-lin},\text{plain-mlp})$ by $\geq 3\%$ in $\geq 2$ cells at
        $N\leq1500$, seed-consistent'') were fixed \emph{before} launching the runs,
        following~\cite{schnabel2025}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Training details (all arms).} AdamW, learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay
$10^{-4}$, cosine schedule, label smoothing $0.05$, batch $128$, $20$ epochs, 9:1
train/val split. Cross-entropy on the three classes (axion / CDM / no-substructure).
Hardware/software: NVIDIA H200; PennyLane \texttt{default.qubit} with
\texttt{backprop} (exact statevector, no shot noise). MAE-ViT additionally receives
its \emph{full} recipe (mask-$0.9$ pretraining on \emph{no\_sub}, Adam $5\times10^{-5}$,
$50$ epochs, rot90/flip augmentation)---i.e.\ every advantage its paper prescribes.

\subsection{Statistical significance}
\label{sec:significance}

To make a defensible inferential claim we replicated the two signal cells
(Model\_II, $N\in\{250,500\}$) to $n{=}10$ seeds (seeds $42$--$51$, job 200728)
and ran, per cell, a paired one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test for both the
quantum-over-plain and quantum-over-sham contrasts, with Holm family-wise
correction over the six tests. Table~\ref{tab:signif} gives the result.
\textbf{At $n{=}10$ under Holm correction the quantum-specific effect is only
marginal}: the strongest cell (Model\_II $N{=}250$, Q$>$sham) reaches
$p_{\mathrm{Holm}}{=}0.059$, and the $3$-seed Q$-$sham $+1.6\%$ at
(Model\_II, $N{=}500$) shrank to $+0.002$ at $n{=}10$---partly seed luck exposed
by the larger replication. Because $(\text{II},250)$ Q$>$sham was the pre-specified strongest
cell, we had pre-registered a two-stage sequential extension of exactly this
contrast to $n{=}20$ at $\alpha{=}0.01$ (disclosed as sequential).
\textbf{Stage 2 confirms it}: at $n{=}20$ (seeds $42$--$61$, job 200800) the
quantum-over-sham contribution at $(\text{II},250)$ is $+0.0140$ ($95\%$ CI
$[+0.0048,+0.0233]$, $15/20$ positive), one-sided paired Wilcoxon
$p{=}0.0032 < \alpha{=}0.01$---\emph{statistically significant}. The
quantum-over-\emph{plain} contrast at the same cell remains non-significant
($+0.0081$, CI $[-0.0010,+0.0172]$, $11/20$, $p{=}0.088$). The confirmed effect is
therefore a \emph{module-level} statement (circuit vs.\ matched classical layer);
it is small ($+1.4\%$, below our $3\%$ practical bar); and---per the entanglement
ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ent-hybrid})---it comes from the amplitude-embedding
structure and multi-basis readout, not the entangling ring. Its direction
independently replicates on Model\_III at $N{=}250$ ($n{=}3$;
Section~\ref{sec:generalization}).

\subsection{Entanglement ablation: is it the circuit or just the structure?}
\label{sec:entanglement}

module-level quantum claim isolates \emph{entanglement} at fixed parameter shape:
same architecture, same $96$-angle budget ($4$ layers $\times$ $8$ qubits
$\times$ $3$ angles), same AmplitudeEmbedding and $\langle Z\rangle$ readout, but
per-qubit \texttt{Rot} only (\textbf{NOENT}), with \emph{no} CNOT ring. The three
arms are ENT (full SEL), NOENT (Rot-only), and SHAM (Linear$+$Tanh).

NOENT$-$SHAM $=+0.037$); at $N{=}500$ the structure term dominates
(ENT$-$NOENT only $+0.005$); at $N{=}8000$ all three arms converge.

as well as large ($+22$--$28$ AUC points), even though we attribute it to the CNN
inductive-bias family rather than to the circuit.

\subsection{Negative results}
\label{sec:negative}

We regard the following failures as first-class findings: they bound the claim and
prevent the over-reading that the benchmarking literature warns against.

\paragraph{(a) Qubit scaling does not transfer to the residual architecture.}
On the \emph{scratch} (forced-bottleneck) architecture, widening the Hilbert space
($N_Q: 8\to12$) amplified the quantum-over-sham margin (up to $+0.162$ at
Model\_I, $N{=}1000$)---a result the earlier draft advertised as a scaling law.
On the \emph{hybrid} architecture the same lever is dead
(Table~\ref{tab:qubit-neg}): Model\_II $N{=}500$ is \emph{identical} at
$N_Q{=}8$ and $12$ ($0.9216 = 0.9216$), Model\_II $N{=}250$ is slightly worse,
and on Model\_I the $N_Q{=}12$ \emph{sham} ($0.8471$) \emph{beats} the

\section{Analysis: What the Quantum Branch Can and Cannot Do}
\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{A data-processing constraint.}
The quantum branch output $\mathbf{m}$ is a \emph{deterministic} function of the
CNN feature $\mathbf{h}$: $\mathbf{m} = g(\mathbf{h})$ with
$g = \langle Z\rangle \circ U_{\theta} \circ \mathrm{NAE}$. For any label $Y$, the
data-processing inequality~\cite{cover2006} gives
\begin{equation}
  I(Y; \mathbf{h}) \;\geq\; I\big(Y; g(\mathbf{h})\big) = I(Y; \mathbf{m}),
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  I\big(Y; (\mathbf{h}, \mathbf{m})\big) = I(Y; \mathbf{h}),
\end{equation}
the last equality because $\mathbf{m}$ is a function of $\mathbf{h}$. \textbf{The
quantum branch adds no label information the CNN has not already extracted.}
Whatever it does must therefore be \emph{geometric}: it changes the coordinate
system the linear head reads, not the information content.

\paragraph{Why a geometric prior helps at low $N$ and is redundant at high $N$.}
Re-expressing $\mathbf{h}$ through the NAE$\to$unitary$\to$expectation map imposes
a structured, isometry-flavored coordinate (amplitudes live on the unit sphere
$\mathcal{S}^{255}$; $U_\theta\in SU(256)$ preserves it). When labeled data is
scarce (few anchor points to fix the decision geometry), this prior reduces
variance and is worth its bias---exactly the $+2\%$ we see at $N\in\{250,500\}$ on
Model\_II. When data is abundant, the CNN features already determine the geometry
and the extra coordinate is redundant, so the margin decays to zero
(Tables~\ref{tab:hybrid-II}--\ref{tab:hybrid-I}). This is precisely the behavior a
data-processing-limited regularizer must exhibit, and it is consistent with the
Caro small-$T$ generalization picture~\cite{caro2022} without requiring any
quantum-advantage claim.

\paragraph{The decisive mechanism check: forced vs.\ hybrid at the peak cell.}
The same $(\text{dataset}, N)$ cell settles the question. On the forced-bottleneck
architecture, Model\_II $N{=}1500$ gave a quantum-over-sham margin of
$\mathbf{+0.108}$ (three-arm rerun; $+0.112$ in the original sweep). On the
residual hybrid, the identical cell gives $\mathbf{+0.001}$
(Table~\ref{tab:hybrid-II}). The $100\times$ collapse is the signature of
compensation, not information: the large module-level margin existed \emph{only}
while the network was forced through the $8$-dim bottleneck, where the circuit was
recovering a handicap the linear sham could not. Once $\mathbf{h}$ bypasses the
bottleneck, there is nothing left for the circuit to recover, and the honest
residual contribution is the small low-$N$ prior above. This is the cleanest
in-house demonstration of the null-baseline lesson of~\cite{bowles2024}.

\paragraph{Entanglement follows the same two-stage logic.}
The entanglement ablation makes the identical point at the level of the circuit's
internal structure, and this is---we believe---the most scientifically informative
finding in the study. On the \emph{forced-readout} architecture the entangling
ring is \emph{load-bearing}: ENT $>$ NOENT $>$ SHAM in $6/6$ three-arm cells, and
at the peak cell (Model\_II, $N{=}1500$) entanglement supplies $\approx 66\%$ of
the quantum-over-sham margin (Table~\ref{tab:ent-scratch}). On the \emph{residual}
architecture the same ablation collapses to ENT $\approx$ NOENT
(Table~\ref{tab:ent-hybrid}: $n{=}10$ deltas $+0.0022$ and $-0.0000$, both
non-significant). The synthesis is exactly the data-processing story above:
entanglement's apparent contribution in the forced setting was \emph{compensation}
for information the $8$-dimensional bottleneck destroyed---the entangling ring
helped reconstruct a usable representation from a crippled one. Once $\mathbf{h}$
reaches the head directly there is no lost information to reconstruct, and all
that remains of the quantum branch is a structural prior (amplitude-embedding
geometry $+$ multi-basis readout) that neither the entangling ring nor extra
qubits (Section~\ref{sec:negative}) improve. Entanglement matters exactly when,
and because, the architecture is handicapped, and stops mattering when it is
not---fully consistent with the impossibility of adding label information
downstream of $\mathbf{h}$.

\paragraph{Training audit (the circuit is genuinely trained).}
Lest the small margins be read as an untrained circuit: direct instrumentation
shows non-zero circuit gradient norms ($0.12$--$0.32$, no barren
plateau~\cite{mcclean2018barren}), substantial weight drift
($\|\theta-\theta_0\|: 0.08\to8.5$), informative outputs
($\sigma(\langle Z\rangle) = 0.05$--$0.09$), and---on the scratch architecture
where the circuit is the sole pathway---AUC collapses to exactly $0.5000$ when the
circuit is zeroed. The circuit is trained, used, and decisive; it simply computes
a function a matched classical layer can also compute.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Simulator only.} All quantum results use exact-statevector
        simulation ($\leq16$ qubits, \texttt{default.qubit}, \texttt{backprop});
        we do not report NISQ-hardware runs. The angle-encoding variant is the
        hardware-realistic path but is itself only simulated here.
  \item \textbf{Magnitude below the practical threshold.} Although the
        quantum-over-sham contribution at $(\text{Model\_II}, N{=}250)$ is
        statistically significant ($n{=}20$, $p{=}0.0032$), its magnitude
        ($+1.4\%$ AUC) is \emph{below} our $3\%$ practical-significance bar. We do
        not claim quantum advantage.
  \item \textbf{Single MAE encoder.} The paradigm comparison uses one
        MAE-pretrained ViT recipe~\cite{prasha2025masked}; a different
        self-supervised encoder or a much larger pretraining corpus could shift
        the low-data collapse we observe.
  \item \textbf{Scope of the significance claim.} The confirmed module-level
        effect is single-cell: only the quantum-over-sham contrast at
        $(\text{Model\_II}, N{=}250)$ reaches significance; the quantum-over-plain
        contrast there ($p{=}0.088$) and the other Stage-1 cells at $n{=}10$ do
        not (Table~\ref{tab:signif}). We make no broader module-level claim than
        this one cell.
  \item \textbf{Sequential design disclosed.} Significance was established with a
        pre-registered two-stage sequential procedure (Stage-1 $n{=}10$, then
        Stage-2 $n{=}20$ at $\alpha{=}0.01$ on the pre-specified strongest cell).
        We disclose the sequential testing explicitly so the inference is
        transparent; the confirmatory $\alpha$ was fixed before Stage 2.
  \item \textbf{CommonTest budget.} The fourth-dataset result is a
        boundary characterization at a fixed compute budget, not a tuned benchmark;
        the underfit regime prevents any branch-level conclusion there.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We set out to determine, under the fair-comparison standards the field now
requires, whether a small quantum circuit helps on gravitational-lensing
dark-matter classification---and to report the answer honestly, whichever way it
came out. The answer is nuanced and, we believe, more useful than a headline
advantage claim.

QVF-Hybrid is a guaranteed-floor residual architecture: zeroing its quantum block
recovers the plain-CNN classifier exactly (Proposition~\ref{prop:floor}), so the
strongest classical null baseline is a lower bound by construction. Against a
parameter-matched sham, the residual quantum branch delivers a module-level
contribution that is \emph{statistically significant} at the strongest cell
(Model\_II, $N{=}250$; quantum vs.\ sham, pre-registered two-stage design,
$n{=}20$, $p{=}0.0032 < \alpha{=}0.01$) yet small ($+1.4\%$ AUC, below our $3\%$
practical bar) and, per the entanglement ablation, driven by amplitude-embedding
structure rather than the entangling ring---which is load-bearing in the
forced-readout architecture ($6/6$ strict ordering) but reduces to
ENT $\approx$ NOENT once the feature bypasses the bottleneck. Against the MAE-ViT
SOTA, the hybrid clears the pre-registered $\geq3\%$ bar in $10/11$ cells across
three datasets ($3$-seed, $3/3$ seed-paired, $+3.1$ to $+32.0$ AUC points), and
on three representative low-$N$ cells this win is \emph{statistically significant}
(Holm $p{=}0.003$, $10/10$ seed-paired, $+22$--$28$ AUC points), with $1/19$ the
parameters and zero pretraining data---though we attribute this to the CNN
inductive-bias family, of which the hybrid is the strongest variant. A data-processing argument explains the whole
pattern: the quantum branch cannot add label information, only reshape geometry,
which is worth a small variance reduction at low $N$ and redundant at high $N$;
the $100\times$ collapse of the peak margin from the forced to the residual
architecture confirms the earlier large margins were bottleneck compensation, not
a quantum information gain.

For survey astronomy, the practical takeaway is a design principle rather than a
hardware recommendation: in the few-hundred-labels-per-class regime relevant to
early survey phases, a compact from-scratch CNN family sharply outperforms a
pretrained ViT, and a residual quantum readout can add a small but statistically
significant low-data prior at no floor risk---while the honest magnitude of that
quantum contribution remains below the threshold at which quantum hardware
investment would be justified on this task. We release the full control ladder,
the negative results, and the pre-registered significance tests so that the
boundary of the claim is as legible as its interior.

\paragraph{Broader impact.}
This work quantifies \emph{when} a near-term quantum circuit does and does not
provide measurable benefit over classical models on astrophysical imaging,
contributing to a rigorous accounting of where quantum hardware investment is
scientifically justified. No dual-use or harmful applications are anticipated.
