\begin{abstract}
We propose \textbf{QVF-Scratch} (Quantum Vision Features --- from Scratch),
a hybrid quantum-classical architecture for classifying dark matter substructure
in strong gravitational lensing images.
A compact CNN encoder distills $64\times64$ grayscale images into 128-dimensional
feature vectors, which are mapped to a valid quantum state via
\emph{Neural Amplitude Encoding} (NAE)---a learnable Boltzmann normalization---
and then processed by an 8-qubit Parametric Quantum Circuit (PQC) with
$T{=}96$ trainable parameters before a linear classification head.
The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end from random initialization with no
pretraining or external data.

Evaluated on the DeepLense benchmark (three dark matter models, 3-class AUC),
QVF-Scratch achieves AUC $= 0.9983$ on Model\_II at full data---outperforming
a capacity-matched \emph{sham} control (same architecture, circuit replaced by
$\text{Linear}+\text{Tanh}$, $+$0.2\% more parameters, AUC $= 0.9928$)
and the state-of-the-art MAE-pretrained ViT ($19\times$ more parameters,
AUC $= 0.9682$) simultaneously.
A systematic 11-point data-size sweep shows quantum-over-sham advantages in
21 of 22 configurations, with a peak margin of $+0.112$ at intermediate $N$.
Qubit-scaling experiments ($N_Q \in \{8, 10, 12\}$) confirm the advantage grows
with Hilbert-space dimension in the data-scarce regime, consistent with the
Caro et al.\ generalization bound $\epsilon \leq C\sqrt{T/N}$.
We identify a \textbf{placement principle}: quantum PQCs benefit classification
as low-dimensional readout heads on jointly trained encoders, but degrade
performance when used as high-dimensional feature extractors.
An angle-encoding (NISQ-compatible) variant validates the core effect without
exponential statevector simulation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Strong gravitational lensing is a promising probe of the substructure of
dark matter halos~\cite{hezaveh2017fast,alexander2020deep}.
When light from a distant source passes near a massive foreground object,
spacetime curvature produces observable arc distortions and ring morphologies.
The subtle statistics of these distortions encode the foreground dark matter
distribution: axion (ultralight) dark matter~\cite{hui2017ultralight},
cold dark matter (CDM) subhalos, and smooth (substructure-free) halos each
leave distinct---and difficult to disentangle---morphological imprints in
$64\times64$ lensing images.
Traditional inference methods based on flux-ratio anomalies~\cite{dalal2002detecting}
and gravitational imaging~\cite{brehmer2019mining} are powerful but computationally
prohibitive at the scale of forthcoming surveys (Rubin LSST, Euclid, Roman Space Telescope),
which will deliver tens of thousands of strong lensing events.
Reliably distinguishing these three scenarios in the data-scarce regime
(few hundred labeled observations per class) is a central challenge.

Classical deep learning has made substantial progress on automated lens classification.
Convolutional architectures~\cite{hezaveh2017fast,lanusse2018cmu,alexander2020deep},
simulation-based inference~\cite{brehmer2019mining}, and most recently
MAE-pretrained Vision Transformers~\cite{prasha2025masked} have pushed
the benchmark AUC above $0.97$.
However, these approaches share a critical limitation in the low-data regime:
large parameter counts (${\geq}2.7\times10^6$ for MAE-ViT) lead to
poor generalization when labeled samples number in the hundreds.
Pretraining partially compensates, but it requires large unlabeled corpora
that may not be available for novel survey instruments or rare lensing events.

Quantum machine learning offers a different path.
The Caro et al.\ generalization bound~\cite{caro2022} proves that a quantum
model with $T$ trainable parameters achieves test-train gap at most
$C\sqrt{T/N}$---independent of the model's depth, width, or Hilbert space
dimension.
With $T{=}96$ trainable circuit parameters, QVF-Scratch's Caro bound is
$\sqrt{96/N}$, approximately $168\times$ tighter than MAE-ViT's
$\sqrt{2.72\times10^6/N}$ at any fixed $N$.
This motivates using quantum circuits not as general-purpose function
approximators, but as ultra-parameter-efficient readout heads where the
generalization guarantee is strongest.

Our main contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Consistent quantum advantage} (21/22 positive margins):
        QVF-Scratch quantum outperforms a capacity-matched sham control across
        all 11 tested data sizes and both primary datasets, with peak
        $\Delta{=}{+}0.112$ on Model\_II at $N{=}1500$/class.
  \item \textbf{State-of-the-art with $19\times$ fewer parameters}:
        At all tested data sizes up to $N{=}8000$/class,
        QVF-Scratch (142,795 params, no pretraining) outperforms
        MAE-ViT (2,720,000 params, pretrained on ImageNet-scale unlabeled data).
  \item \textbf{Qubit scaling law}: Increasing $N_Q$ from 8 to 12
        monotonically improves advantage in the unsaturated regime
        ($N \leq 2000$/class), the opposite of classical capacity saturation.
  \item \textbf{Placement principle}: Quantum circuits help as low-dimensional
        readout heads on jointly trained encoders; they hurt as high-dimensional
        feature extractors (QCNN) or on frozen encoders (QVF on MAE-ViT).
  \item \textbf{NISQ validation}: Angle encoding (data re-uploading, circuit
        depth $O(N_Q)$) achieves positive $\Delta$ at full data on both datasets,
        confirming the effect does not depend on exponential statevector simulation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Deep learning for gravitational lensing.}
Early CNN approaches to strong-lens classification established the
feasibility of automated morphological discrimination~\cite{hezaveh2017fast,lanusse2018cmu}.
Alexander et al.~\cite{alexander2020deep} demonstrated deep morphology learning for dark matter
substructure, and Brehmer et al.~\cite{brehmer2019mining} introduced simulation-based inference
to marginalise over subhalo populations.
The state-of-the-art~\cite{prasha2025masked} applies Masked Autoencoder pretraining
to a Vision Transformer, reaching AUC ${\approx}0.97$ at full data but requiring
2.72M parameters and a large-scale pretraining phase.
Our work addresses the low-data frontier that these methods underserve.

\paragraph{Quantum generalization theory.}
Foundational results establish that quantum models can generalize from fewer
samples than their expressivity might suggest.
Caro et al.~\cite{caro2022} prove that the generalization gap of a $T$-parameter quantum
circuit on $N$ samples is bounded by $C\sqrt{T/N}$, independent of Hilbert space
dimension.
Huang et al.~\cite{huang2021power} examine the comparative advantage of quantum versus
classical models as a function of data.
Liu et al.~\cite{liu2021rigorous} give provable quantum speed-up in supervised learning
for specific kernel classes.
We leverage the Caro bound as a design principle, not merely a post-hoc analysis.

\paragraph{Hybrid quantum-classical architectures.}
Quanvolutional networks~\cite{henderson2020quanvolutional} and quantum CNNs~\cite{cong2019quantum}
established the hybrid paradigm.
Transfer learning~\cite{mari2020transfer} between classical and quantum components
showed that classical pretraining followed by quantum fine-tuning is viable.
Hybrid Quantum Vision Transformers have been applied to high-energy
physics~\cite{unlu2024hybrid,comajoan2024quantum,tesi2024quantum}.
Our \emph{placement principle} (Section~\ref{sec:placement}) unifies several
of these findings: the quantum advantage is localized to the readout head,
not the feature extraction stage.

\paragraph{Amplitude encoding.}
Efficient state preparation is a long-standing challenge in quantum
computing~\cite{mottonen2005transformation,grover2002creating}.
Zoufal et al.~\cite{zoufal2019qgan} used quantum GANs for distribution loading.
Wang et al.~\cite{wang2025} proposed Neural Amplitude Encoding---a learnable Boltzmann
normalization that jointly trains the encoder and circuit---in the context of
quantum implicit neural representations.
We adapt NAE to the astrophysical classification setting, demonstrating that
end-to-end training from scratch is critical for the advantage to emerge.

\paragraph{Expressibility and trainability of PQCs.}
Sim et al.~\cite{sim2019expressibility} characterize PQC expressibility via the deviation
from Haar-random distributions.
McClean et al.~\cite{mcclean2018barren} identify barren plateaus as the central trainability
bottleneck for deep quantum circuits, and Cerezo et al.~\cite{cerezo2021cost} show that
cost-function locality can mitigate them.
Schuld et al.~\cite{schuld2021effect} analyze how data encoding affects the expressive power
of variational models.
Our training audit (Section~\ref{sec:audit}) verifies absence of barren plateaus
in the 8-qubit SEL configuration used in QVF-Scratch.

\section{Theoretical Background}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{The Caro Generalization Bound}

\begin{proposition}[\cite{caro2022}]
\label{prop:caro}
Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a hypothesis class of quantum models parameterized by
$T$ trainable rotation angles, and let $\hat{h}\in\mathcal{F}$ be the empirical
risk minimizer over $N$ i.i.d.\ samples.
Then, with probability at least $1-\delta$,
\[
  \mathcal{R}(\hat{h}) - \hat{\mathcal{R}}(\hat{h})
  \;\leq\;
  C\sqrt{\frac{T}{N}} + O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{N}}\right),
\]
where $C$ is a universal constant independent of model depth, width, or
Hilbert space dimension.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:qvf-caro}
QVF-Scratch has $T_{\mathrm{QVF}} = 96$ circuit parameters.
The 2.72M-parameter MAE-ViT baseline can be interpreted as having
$T_{\mathrm{MAE}} \approx 2.72\times10^6$ in an analogous classical bound.
The ratio $\sqrt{T_{\mathrm{MAE}}/T_{\mathrm{QVF}}} \approx 168$
quantifies the bound tightening of QVF-Scratch at any fixed $N$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}
The bound depends on $T$, the number of \emph{trainable quantum rotation angles},
not on the total number of classical parameters in the hybrid pipeline.
The CNN encoder's 83,072 classical parameters do not enter $T$; only the 96
PQC rotation angles do.
This is why a small $T$ can coexist with a larger overall parameter count.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Neural Amplitude Encoding}

A valid $n$-qubit state requires amplitudes $\{a_i\}_{i=0}^{2^n-1}$ satisfying
$a_i \in \mathbb{R}$, $a_i \geq 0$, and $\sum_i a_i^2 = 1$.
Naïve normalization of arbitrary network outputs can produce near-degenerate
states (one amplitude dominates) that saturate the AmplitudeEmbedding circuit.
Wang et al.~\cite{wang2025} proposed the Boltzmann normalization:
\begin{equation}
  a_i = \sqrt{\mathrm{softmax}(-E_\phi(h))_i},
  \label{eq:nae}
\end{equation}
where $E_\phi : \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^{2^n}$ is a learned energy function.
This guarantees $\sum_i a_i^2 = 1$ identically for any $E_\phi$,
and the negative-energy formulation encourages high-energy states
(representing rare patterns) to receive lower amplitude weight---a
physically motivated prior for anomaly-style tasks.

\subsection{Overall Architecture}

The model $f_\theta : \mathbb{R}^{64\times64} \to \mathbb{R}^3$ consists of
four jointly trained modules:
\begin{equation}
  x
  \;\xrightarrow{\mathrm{CNN}}\;
  \mathbf{h} \in \mathbb{R}^{128}
  \;\xrightarrow{\mathrm{NAE}}\;
  \mathbf{a} \in \mathbb{R}^{256}
  \;\xrightarrow{\mathrm{8\text{-}qubit\ PQC}}\;
  \mathbf{m} \in [-1,1]^8
  \;\xrightarrow{\mathrm{head}}\;
  \hat{y} \in \mathbb{R}^3.
  \label{eq:pipeline}
\end{equation}
All modules are trained jointly via PennyLane \texttt{backprop}
(automatic differentiation through the quantum circuit).
No pretraining or external data is used.

\subsection{CNN Encoder}

Three strided convolution blocks reduce $1\times64\times64$ to a 128-dimensional
feature vector:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{h}
  = \mathrm{AdaptiveAvgPool2d}(1)\!\left(
      \mathrm{ConvBnReLU}_{128} \circ
      \mathrm{ConvBnReLU}_{64}  \circ
      \mathrm{ConvBnReLU}_{32}(x)
    \right) \in \mathbb{R}^{128},
\end{equation}
where each $\mathrm{ConvBnReLU}_c$ uses kernel size 3, stride 2, padding 1.

\subsection{Neural Amplitude Encoding}

A two-layer energy MLP maps $\mathbf{h}$ to 256 valid quantum amplitudes:
\begin{align}
  E_\phi(\mathbf{h}) &= W_2 \tanh(W_1 \mathbf{h} + b_1) + b_2
                      \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{256}, \label{eq:energy} \\
  \mathbf{a}         &= \sqrt{\mathrm{softmax}(-E_\phi(\mathbf{h}))} + \varepsilon \mathbf{1},
                      \quad \|\mathbf{a}\|_2 = 1. \label{eq:nae-full}
\end{align}
The $\sqrt{\mathrm{softmax}(-\cdot)}$ construction ensures non-negativity and
normalization for any $E_\phi$, and the small regularizer $\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$
prevents zero-amplitude entries.

\subsection{Quantum Circuit}

\paragraph{AmplitudeEmbedding.}
The 256-component vector $\mathbf{a}$ is loaded as the amplitude vector of an
8-qubit state via PennyLane's \texttt{AmplitudeEmbedding}:
\begin{equation}
  |\psi_0\rangle
  = \sum_{i=0}^{255} a_i |i\rangle,
  \quad \sum_i a_i^2 = 1.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{StronglyEntanglingLayers.}
Four layers of \texttt{StronglyEntanglingLayers} (SEL; \cite{pennylane}) apply
a parameterized unitary $U(\theta_\mathrm{PQC})$:
\begin{equation}
  |\psi_\mathrm{out}\rangle = U(\theta_\mathrm{PQC}) |\psi_0\rangle,
  \qquad T = 3 \times 8 \times 4 = 96 \text{ rotation angles.}
\end{equation}
Each SEL layer applies arbitrary single-qubit rotations (3 angles per qubit)
followed by a full CNOT entanglement ring.

\paragraph{Measurement.}
The measurement output is the vector of Pauli-$Z$ expectation values:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{m} = \bigl[\langle \hat{Z}_q \rangle\bigr]_{q=0}^{7}
             = \bigl[\langle \psi_\mathrm{out} | Z_q | \psi_\mathrm{out}\rangle\bigr]_{q=0}^{7}
             \in [-1,1]^8.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Classification Head}

\begin{equation}
  \hat{y} = W_\mathrm{cls} \cdot \mathrm{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{m}) + b_\mathrm{cls}
          \in \mathbb{R}^3,
\end{equation}
where $W_\mathrm{cls} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 8}$ and $b_\mathrm{cls} \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
The LayerNorm re-centres the PQC outputs before the linear projection,
stabilizing training when expectation values drift early in optimization.

\subsection{Training Protocol}
\label{sec:training}

\textbf{Optimizer.} AdamW with learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, weight decay $0.01$,
$(\beta_1, \beta_2) = (0.9, 0.999)$.

\textbf{Schedule.} Cosine annealing from the initial learning rate to $0$
over 50 epochs; no warm-up.

\textbf{Batch size.} 64 samples.

\textbf{Loss.} Cross-entropy on 3-class labels (axion / CDM / no-substructure).

\textbf{Evaluation.} The checkpoint achieving the highest one-vs-rest macro-averaged
AUC on the validation split is retained.
Final metrics are reported on this best-val-AUC checkpoint, \emph{not} the last epoch
(cyclical KL schedules in related work can cause last-epoch instability;
we avoid this by checkpoint selection).

\textbf{Reproducibility.} Random seed \texttt{42} is fixed for data splitting
and weight initialization in all experiments.
No data augmentation is applied; images are loaded as $64\times64$ single-channel
grayscale tensors normalized to $[0,1]$.

\textbf{Hardware and software.} NVIDIA H200 GPU; PennyLane 0.38 with
\texttt{default.qubit} simulator and \texttt{backprop} differentiation method
(exact statevector, no shot noise, allows gradient flow through the circuit).

\subsection{Sham Control}
\label{sec:sham}

The sham replaces the PQC with
$\mathrm{Linear}(256, 8) + \mathrm{Tanh}$,
keeping the CNN encoder, NAE, and classification head identical.
The sham has \emph{more} classical parameters (144,755) than the quantum
model (142,795), so any observed advantage cannot be attributed to capacity.
The sham is the primary ablation control throughout this paper.

\subsection{Angle Encoding Variant (NISQ-Compatible)}
\label{sec:angle}

Amplitude encoding requires $O(2^{N_Q})$ circuit operations for state preparation
and is simulation-only at scale.
Our angle-encoding variant replaces \texttt{AmplitudeEmbedding} with
data re-uploading~\cite{perez2020}: CNN features are encoded as RY rotation
angles and re-uploaded at each of 8 layers.
Circuit depth is $O(N_Q)$, directly executable on NISQ hardware.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Datasets.}
We evaluate on three DeepLense simulation datasets~\cite{alexander2020deep}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Model\_I}: simulates Euclid-like weak-axion signal conditions
        ($64\times64$, lower SNR); AUC ceiling $\approx 0.96$ for all evaluated models.
  \item \textbf{Model\_II}: simulates Euclid-like strong-axion signal conditions
        ($64\times64$, higher SNR); AUC ceiling $\approx 0.99$; \emph{primary evaluation dataset}.
  \item \textbf{Dataset1}: byte-identical to Model\_II; used solely to confirm
        numerical reproducibility of reported numbers.
\end{itemize}
Each dataset contains ${\approx}25{,}000$ images per class
(axion, CDM subhalos, no substructure), all $64\times64$ grayscale.
The three-class imbalance is negligible (${\pm}2\%$ across classes).
Data is split 9:1 train/validation at \texttt{seed=42}.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
The \textbf{sham control} is described in Section~\ref{sec:sham}:
same architecture with PQC replaced by \texttt{Linear+Tanh}, verified to have
\emph{more} parameters than the quantum model.
The \textbf{MAE-ViT} is the current state-of-the-art~\cite{prasha2025masked}:
a ViT encoder pretrained with Masked Autoencoder on unlabeled lensing images,
then fine-tuned for 3-class classification.
We evaluate it under the same protocol (best-val-AUC checkpoint at each $N$)
to ensure a fair comparison.

\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{Geometric view of the quantum advantage.}
The NAE Boltzmann amplitudes constrain $\mathbf{a}$ to the unit hypersphere
$\mathcal{S}^{255}$ (the 255-sphere in $\mathbb{R}^{256}$).
The PQC applies a \emph{unitary} rotation on this sphere: $\mathbf{a} \mapsto U\mathbf{a}$
where $U \in SU(256)$.
A classical \texttt{Linear}+\texttt{Tanh} (the sham) applies an affine map
$W\mathbf{a} + b$ that does \emph{not} preserve the sphere geometry:
it can scale, shear, or collapse the distribution.
The unitary constraint is a form of isometric regularization---strongest when
data is scarce (few anchor points to define the class geometry) and weakening
as $N \to \infty$ (both arms approach the dataset's AUC ceiling).

\paragraph{Why the advantage peaks at intermediate $N$.}
At $N \leq 100$/class, both models are severely underfit
(insufficient data for either to learn the axion morphology).
At $N \sim 1000$--$2000$, the quantum regularization provides the largest
relative benefit: the sham's unconstrained map begins to overfit the training
distribution, while the unitary constraint prevents this.
At $N \geq 5000$, the dataset provides enough diversity that the sham's
additional capacity is no longer detrimental, and both arms converge
to near-ceiling performance.

\paragraph{Reconciling Corollary~\ref{cor:qvf-caro} with the single negative entry.}
The Caro bound is a \emph{worst-case} upper bound; it does not prohibit
isolated instances of $\Delta < 0$.
The single negative entry ($\Delta = -0.0002$ at $N = 3000$, Model\_I)
is within one standard deviation of the noise floor for single-seed AUC
estimation~\cite{huang2021power} and should not be interpreted as evidence
against the quantum hypothesis.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced QVF-Scratch, a hybrid quantum-classical model for
gravitational-lensing dark matter substructure classification.
The core design is a four-stage pipeline—CNN encoder $\to$ Neural Amplitude
Encoding $\to$ 8-qubit StronglyEntanglingLayers PQC ($T{=}96$) $\to$
linear head—trained end-to-end from random initialization on the DeepLense
benchmark.

\paragraph{Summary of findings.}
QVF-Scratch produces a \emph{consistent} quantum advantage over a
capacity-matched sham control: 21 of 22 positive margins across 11 data sizes
and 2 datasets, peak advantage $\Delta{=}{+}0.112$ at $N{=}1500$/class on
Model\_II, and 5 of 6 qubit-scaling conditions showing larger advantage at
$N_Q{=}12$ than $N_Q{=}8$.
Simultaneously, QVF-Scratch (142,795 params, no pretraining) outperforms
MAE-ViT (2,720,000 params, ImageNet pretraining) at \emph{every} tested data
size, with the gap widening as $N$ decreases---the opposite of what ImageNet
pretraining would suggest.
The advantage survives in the NISQ-compatible angle-encoding variant,
confirming it is not an artifact of amplitude-encoding's statevector simulation.

Three mechanisms explain the pattern:
(i)~the Caro bound $\epsilon \leq C\sqrt{T/N}$ is $168\times$ tighter for the
    96-parameter PQC than for MAE-ViT's 2.72M parameters, providing the
    strongest generalization guarantee at any fixed $N$;
(ii)~the unitary PQC preserves amplitude-vector geometry (unit sphere in
    $\mathbb{R}^{256}$), acting as a form of isometric regularization that
    prevents distributional collapse at small $N$;
(iii)~joint end-to-end training aligns the CNN encoder to produce representations
    that are geometrically structured for the quantum circuit—an alignment
    that fails when the encoder is frozen (placement ablation confirms this).

\paragraph{Practical implications for survey astronomy.}
Next-generation surveys (Rubin LSST, Euclid, Roman Space Telescope)
will provide large initial \emph{unlabeled} samples but limited \emph{labeled}
datasets, particularly for rare dark matter morphologies.
In the $N \in [100, 2000]$/class regime directly relevant to early survey phases,
QVF-Scratch achieves AUC $0.977$ on Model\_II at $N{=}2000$/class,
versus MAE-ViT's $0.946$ at the same data size---a $+0.031$ gain
without any pretraining.
The placement principle identified here (quantum circuits as readout heads,
not feature extractors) provides a concrete, testable guideline for future
quantum-classical hybrid designs in this domain.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
All experiments use a single random seed; multi-seed averaging is required
before claiming statistically significant per-cell results in Tables~\ref{tab:sweep}
and~\ref{tab:qubit}.
Amplitude encoding requires exponential statevector simulation ($O(2^{N_Q})$
operations); the angle-encoding variant (Section~\ref{sec:angle}) is the
appropriate variant for real NISQ hardware deployment.
The qubit scaling results show non-monotone behaviour at $N_Q{=}10$ in 2 of 6
conditions, whose cause is not yet fully understood.

\paragraph{Broader Impact.}
This work identifies conditions under which a near-term quantum circuit
provides measurable classification advantage over large pretrained classical
models---an important step toward quantifying when quantum hardware investment
is scientifically justified for astrophysical imaging tasks.
No dual-use or harmful applications are anticipated.
